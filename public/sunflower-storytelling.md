## 1. MANIFESTO — "About Sunflower"

### A flower that turns toward the light — not toward whoever is holding it

A sunflower doesn't wait to be told which way the sun is. It turns on its own — *heliotropism*, the botanists call it: an organism orienting itself toward the one source that actually sustains it, ignoring everything else that flickers around it.

We named this app after that instinct, because it's the instinct we think has gone missing from how people meet information today.

Somewhere in the last twenty years, the direction got outsourced. An algorithm decided what you'd see. A feed decided what mattered today. And now, increasingly, a chatbot decides what the "answer" is — one shaped quietly by everything you've ever typed into it, agreeing with you a little more each time, because agreement is what keeps you talking.

None of these are neutral. All of them are trying to hold the flower and point it themselves.

**Sunflower turns the stem back over to you.**

### The problem isn't that we lack information. It's that we're drowning in it.

We are the most informed generation in human history, and also one of the most confused. Not because the facts aren't out there — they are, more of them than ever — but because information has been decoupled from understanding. We scroll, we absorb, we react, and by tomorrow it's gone, replaced by the next wave. Nothing settles. Nothing becomes ours.

This is not a personal failing. It's a design outcome. Attention economies profit from the wave never stopping. A person who *understands* something and moves on is worth less to that system than a person who stays anxious, uncertain, and scrolling.

There's a second failure sitting right behind the first: **we increasingly let something else digest the world for us.** Ask an AI system a question today and you're not getting "the answer" — you're getting *an* answer, quietly bent by your prior questions, your inferred politics, your engagement patterns. It sounds authoritative. It is not neutral. It is a mirror wearing the mask of an oracle, and most people using it don't know that's what they're looking at.

We think this is one of the quiet threats to democratic life. Democracies depend on citizens who can, at minimum, argue from a shared set of facts. When every person's "facts" are individually curated — by a feed, by a chatbot, by an engagement algorithm — shared reality itself starts to fragment. You can't deliberate with someone who was shown, and told, a different world than you were.

### What Sunflower does differently

**We give everyone the same source data.** Not a version of the truth optimized for your profile — the same verified, sourced, checkable information, whoever you are. If AI is used anywhere in Sunflower, it is used as a tool for retrieval, structure, and clarity — never as the origin of the fact itself, and never as a system that adapts what's true depending on who's asking. Facts come from the source. That's not a slogan; it's an architectural decision.

**We help information become knowledge.** Reading a fact is not the same as understanding it. Sunflower borrows from behavioral economics and the science of learning — spaced repetition, active recall, framing that fights our own cognitive shortcuts — to help what you encounter actually stick, connect, and change how you see the next thing you read. The goal isn't more content. It's fewer things, held better.

**We teach by asking, not just by telling.** The oldest method we know for turning information into real understanding is also the simplest: a good question. Socrates never handed his students conclusions — he asked, and let them discover the shape of what they knew and didn't know by trying to answer. Sunflower works the same way. Instead of just delivering a fact and moving on, it asks: *why does this matter? What does this connect to? What would change your mind?* Knowledge that you arrive at by questioning sits differently in the mind than knowledge you were simply told — it's yours because you built it, not because you received it. This is also, quietly, an antidote to the AI-oracle problem: a system that asks you good questions builds a thinking person; a system that hands you confident answers builds a dependent one.

**We unlock data that was already public but never usable.** An enormous amount of the information democracies need — government spending, climate measurements, health statistics, corporate disclosures — is technically open already. It's public record. And it is almost entirely useless to an ordinary person, because it lives caged in spreadsheets, PDFs, and raw datasets built for analysts, not citizens. Open data that nobody can actually read is only open in name. Sunflower takes that caged data and gives it light: turning tables into visualizations, columns into charts, raw numbers into shapes a person can actually see and reason about. The information was always public. Sunflower is the instrument that finally makes it legible.

**We treat your data as belonging to you, not as a product.** Sunflower doesn't build a profile of you to sell to advertisers or to third parties chasing your attention. The only thing your data is used for is showing *you* to *you*: what you actually know, where your gaps are, how your understanding is changing over time. Privacy here isn't a checkbox in a settings menu. It's the whole point — your data works for your self-knowledge, not someone else's revenue.

**We rebuild the room, not just the individual.** Understanding the world alone, in a personalized bubble, is still a bubble — just a bubble of one. Sunflower includes tools for people to think *together*: to compare understanding, discuss, disagree productively, and hold each other accountable to the same facts. And when people are ready to go deeper, Sunflower is a door to real, verified journalists, researchers, and independent think tanks — the humans still doing the difficult, original work of finding things out.

### Why now

Three crises are converging, and they are not separate:

An **ecological crisis** that requires public understanding fast enough to support hard collective decisions — and that gets actively slowed by misinformation, doubt-manufacturing, and the sheer noise of competing "facts."

A **privacy crisis**, where the tools people reach for to make sense of the world are, almost without exception, quietly built to extract from them instead.

A **democratic crisis**, where a shared factual floor — the bare minimum a society needs to argue productively rather than talk past each other — is eroding, replaced by millions of individually tailored realities.

Sunflower is a small, deliberate bet that these three problems share a root: **people have lost direct, undistorted access to how the world actually is.** Give that back, with the tools to turn it into real understanding, and something starts to heal upstream of all three.

### The name, one more time

A sunflower faces the sun not because it's told to, but because that's what it's built to do. It doesn't need someone standing behind it, turning its head.

We'd like to build people the same kind of instrument. Not a filter. Not an oracle. A way of turning, on your own, toward what's real.

## 3. INVESTOR NARRATIVE (Long-form)

### The next trillion-dollar question isn't "how do we generate more content." It's "who do people trust to tell them what's true — and what happens when the answer is no one."

We are living through the fastest collapse of trusted intermediaries in modern history. Traditional media's authority has been eroding for two decades. Social platforms optimized engagement over accuracy and accelerated that erosion. And now generative AI has arrived as the newest, most confident-sounding intermediary yet — one that hallucinates, that reflects the user's own priors back at them, and that a large share of the public is already treating as an oracle rather than a probabilistic text generator.

The result is a market failure with three faces, and most competitors are only solving one of them:

**Face one: information overload with no synthesis layer.** Consumers face infinite content and no reliable mechanism to convert it into durable understanding. Media companies solved distribution. Nobody has solved retention and comprehension at scale.

**Face two: a privacy economy that consumers increasingly distrust.** The dominant model — free product, data as payment, user as product — is now a known, resented pattern. Regulatory pressure (GDPR and its global successors) and consumer sentiment are both moving the same direction: toward products that don't monetize personal data against the user's own interest.

**Face three: a democratic information crisis with real economic downstream costs.** Polarization, an inability to agree on baseline facts, and the erosion of shared reality carry measurable costs — in governance dysfunction, in market volatility around misinformation events, in the growing difficulty of coordinating any large-scale collective action (climate policy chief among them).

**Sunflower is built at the intersection of these three failures, on the thesis that they share a single root cause: individuals have lost direct, unmediated access to verified information, and lost the tools to turn what they do encounter into lasting understanding.**

### Why not just build this with AI?

Because the market is about to be flooded with AI-native information products, and we believe that's a structural weakness, not a strength, for the specific problem we're solving.

Generative AI systems are extraordinary tools — and poor oracles. They hallucinate. They are demonstrably shaped by prior interactions, subtly reinforcing a user's existing beliefs rather than challenging them with the same facts every other user would see. For entertainment, drafting, and brainstorming, this is a minor issue. For a product whose entire value proposition is "understand the world as it actually is," it is disqualifying.

Sunflower's core architectural bet is that **verified-source-first, same-facts-for-everyone** is a durable differentiator precisely because it will *not* be the default posture of AI-native competitors, whose products are built to personalize and to generate rather than to verify and hold constant. We use AI selectively, as infrastructure — retrieval, structuring, personalized *learning pathways* through the same underlying facts — never as the source of the fact itself.

This is a positioning bet with real technical and go-to-market costs (it's slower and more expensive to build on verified sourcing pipelines than to wrap an LLM). We think that cost is exactly what will make it defensible once built.

### The product architecture

Sunflower has three layers:

**1. A verified information layer.** Sourced from established data journalism outlets, research institutions, and think tanks, with transparent provenance on every piece of content. Every user sees the same underlying facts.

**2. A knowledge-formation layer.** Using established behavioral economics and learning-science mechanisms — spaced repetition, active recall, framing effects used *for* comprehension rather than against it — to convert passive reading into retained understanding. This is the layer most competitors skip entirely; most information products stop at delivery.

This layer includes a Socratic questioning engine: rather than terminating an interaction with a delivered fact, Sunflower prompts users toward the next question — why it matters, what it connects to, what would change their mind. This is a deliberate product and defensibility choice, not just a pedagogical flourish. It is the layer where "knowledge, not information" is actually made true or false in the product experience, and it stands in direct contrast to generative-AI products, which are structurally biased toward giving confident answers rather than provoking productive uncertainty.

**2b. An open-data visualization layer.** A large share of the information societies most need — public spending, climate and environmental measurements, health and demographic statistics, corporate and government disclosures — is already legally open. It is simply inaccessible in practice, published as raw tables, PDFs, and datasets designed for analysts, not citizens. This is a specific, addressable market gap: "open" data with near-zero real usability. Sunflower's visualization layer converts this raw public data into charts and visual formats a general user can interpret without training, effectively unlocking an entire existing category of public information that is currently open in name only.

**3. A community and discovery layer.** Tools for users to compare and stress-test their understanding with others, plus a curated discovery surface connecting users directly to independent journalists and think tanks — creating a direct, transparent value flow from reader to source, cutting out the ad-tech intermediary layer that currently captures most of that value today.

### The market

This sits at the convergence of several large, independently growing categories: digital news and media (a market in active business-model crisis, searching for a subscription/trust-based successor to advertising), edtech and lifelong learning, and privacy-first consumer software (a category consistently outperforming ad-supported peers on retention and willingness-to-pay, as consumers grow more sophisticated about the cost of "free").

We are not asking users to trust a new authority. We are asking them to trust a new *process* — one built on the same verified sources they could go check themselves, with none of their data working against them. That is a fundamentally easier trust bar to clear than "trust our AI's judgment," and we think it's the more durable one as AI-generated content increasingly pollutes the rest of the information ecosystem.

There is also an underappreciated adjacent market here: the open-data and civic-data ecosystem. Governments and institutions have spent the last decade publishing enormous volumes of open data under transparency mandates, largely without solving the "last mile" problem of making that data usable by non-specialists. Sunflower's visualization and comprehension layer is directly applicable to this stranded supply of public data — a market created by transparency policy but never actually served by a consumer-facing product.

### The moment

Three converging pressures make this a pre-seed opportunity worth taking seriously right now: the ecological decisions of the next decade require an informed public and are actively undermined by misinformation and noise; consumer trust in ad-funded and AI-native platforms is falling at the same time regulatory and cultural pressure toward privacy-respecting products is rising; and the AI wave itself is producing a growing, underserved segment of people who *specifically* want something that is verified rather than generated, that holds still rather than personalizes the facts, and that respects them rather than extracting from them.

**We're not building another place to consume more. We're building the instrument people use to actually understand what they consume — and to keep that understanding as theirs.**
